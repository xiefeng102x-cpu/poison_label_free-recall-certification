## Decision first

For the paper as it currently exists, **IEEE Transactions on Information
Forensics and Security (TIFS) is the strongest fit-first top-field target**.
The central contribution is an attacker-aware, reproducible security audit of
poisoning detectors across classical and quantum-learning settings, including
negative adaptive and measurement-channel boundaries. TIFS explicitly covers
information forensics and information security and asks authors to make the
information needed for reproducibility available online.

Nature Machine Intelligence (NMI) remains a legitimate but high-risk prestige-first
route because the terminal external-domain block closed as conditional R3 evidence;
the authors would still need to make broad machine-intelligence significance clear without inflating a
conditional benchmark-and-limits result into a universal detector. The present
30-page English research package is not an NMI-formatted Article: the official
Article format permits 3,500 main-text words, a 150-word abstract and up to six
display items. A separate, substantially compressed submission version would
therefore be required.

## Official policy evidence

All pages below were accessed on 2026-10-03.

### TIFS and IEEE Signal Processing Society

- [TIFS scope and reproducible-research page](https://signalprocessingsociety.org/publications-resources/ieee-transactions-information-forensics-and-security): scope includes information forensics and information security; the page encourages online code/data and other information needed to reproduce figures and tables.
- [TIFS deep-learning submission guide](https://signalprocessingsociety.org/sites/default/files/uploads/publications_resources/docs/Guidelines__deep_learning_submissions.pdf): requires explicit architecture, preprocessing, initialization, optimizer, hyperparameters, data order, stopping, split, implementation and version details. It says negative results face a higher reproducibility standard and should demonstrate statistical significance.
- [SPS LLM policy](https://signalprocessingsociety.org/publications-resources/publication-guidelines/policy-on-using-large-language-models-llms): permits language editing, code/visualization acceleration and research/ideation with author responsibility and verification; it forbids an entirely LLM-produced manuscript section and unverified direct use of generated code or text.
- [IEEE AI-generated-content guidance](https://open.ieee.org/author-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-text/): AI-generated content must be disclosed in acknowledgements with the system, affected sections and level of use identified; editing/grammar use is treated separately but disclosure is recommended.

### TDSC and TPAMI

- [TDSC scope](https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/tq/cfp-dependable-secure-computing) and [official topic list](https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/tq/tdsc-topics): include security/dependability design, modeling and evaluation, threat assessment, intrusion detection, benchmarking, attack generation, monitoring, statistical methods, data mining and quantum computing, while retaining a systems-and-networks center.
- [TPAMI scope](https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/tp/cfp-ieee-pattern-analysis-machine-intelligence): centers computer vision, image understanding, pattern analysis/recognition and machine-learning methods used for pattern analysis. The current paper's use of image benchmarks is not itself a new pattern-analysis contribution.

### Nature Machine Intelligence and npj Quantum Information

- [NMI aims and scope](https://www.nature.com/natmachintell/aims): covers high-quality original work across machine learning, deep learning, AI, robotics and their broader scientific/societal implications.
- [NMI content types](https://www.nature.com/natmachintell/content): an Article is a substantial novel research study and is limited to 3,500 main-text words, a 150-word abstract and six display items; an Analysis has the same main-text/display limits and must reach novel conclusions important to a broad audience.
- [NMI submission guide](https://www.nature.com/natmachintell/submission-guidelines): requires authors to check scope, content type, policies and, if selected, double-anonymized-review requirements.
- [npj Quantum Information aims and scope](https://www.nature.com/npjqi/aims): covers all aspects of quantum information, explicitly including quantum computing and quantum machine learning.
- [Nature Portfolio code-sharing guidance](https://support.nature.com/en/support/solutions/articles/6000237619-software-and-code-sharing): calls for Code Availability sections and encourages public code with persistent identifiers and reuse documentation; journal-specific rules still apply.
- [Nature Portfolio AI policy](https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai): human accountability is non-transferable; assistive and interpretive AI uses require proportional verification and transparent disclosure, while opaque replacement of scholarly judgment is not permitted.
