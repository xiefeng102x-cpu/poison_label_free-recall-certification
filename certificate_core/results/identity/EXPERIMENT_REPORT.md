# Trusted-member identity non-interference V1: formal report

## Decision

- Protocol status: `PASS_INDEPENDENT_TRUSTED_IDENTITY_VERIFICATION`
- Scientific primary status: `SUPPORTED`
- Primary cells: 60 supported, 0 inconclusive, 0 contradicted
- Independently recomputed draw-method rows: 490,000

This result supports the preregistered statement that, under the declared
post-training observables and before the defender key is opened, the realized
trusted-member identities are statistically indistinguishable from a secret
uniform draw. It does not establish security after key disclosure, RNG or
process compromise, or a key-dependent side channel.

## Why this is not literally a training-time identity attack

The target trusted-member identities are generated by a newly committed
HMAC-SHA256 key only after victim training, score handoff, preregistration, and
attack-ranking sealing. Consequently, there is no target identity variable to
infer during victim training. An above-chance result in this protocol would
instead diagnose leakage or dependence between the later secret draw and the
attacker's pre-reveal observables.

## Frozen design

- Dataset and victims: PathMNIST, five independently trained ResNet-18 victims
  (seeds 5301--5305)
- Score families: CF-CMD-U, target centroid, cross-fit spectral, activation
  clustering, and QUE score
- Folds: two per victim-detector pair
- Trusted budgets: 120, 500, and 2,000 members
- Primary budget: 2,000 members, 1,000 secret draws per cell
- Attacker corruption budget: 512 candidates
- Non-oracle attacks: public hash, same-detector low score, same-detector high
  score, multi-score extreme, shadow-key frequency, and shadow-key ridge
- Attacker advantage: all five score/flag views, all candidate identifiers,
  victim/fold identifiers, the complete verified-clean candidate universe, the
  public selection algorithm, and 64 public shadow keys
- Excluded information: the defender key, formal target draws, and direct
  key-dependent side channels

The attack runner had no defender-key input and sealed 950 ranking arrays before
the verifier received the key. The frozen attacker-ranking SHA-256 is
`2c0f64cef62ea01f7253beeac512d14c837b983b923be46691f75c0ef2a8e320`.

Across all 60 non-oracle primary cells, the Bonferroni-adjusted confidence
interval envelope is `[0.985166, 1.015286]` for precision lift and
`[0.498114, 0.501825]` for AUROC. Both lie inside the preregistered equivalence
ranges `[0.975, 1.025]` and `[0.49, 0.51]`. The maximum non-oracle mean
trusted-set corruption is 7.9494%, below the frozen 10% ceiling. The oracle-key
control attains AUROC 1 and the maximum possible 25.6% corruption at budget 512
against a 2,000-member trusted set, demonstrating that the pipeline can detect
real identity access.

- Recomputing pooled metrics from per-draw rows agrees with the sealed pooled

- The verifier rechecked all frozen code, configuration, source, pool, ranking,

- Unit tests, runner self-test, and manuscript compilation completed
  successfully.

## Claim boundary

The evidence supports a prospective non-interference claim for a secret,
exchangeable trusted-member draw made after score freeze. It must not be
reported as protection for deployments that designate or specially treat a
fixed trusted subset before training. Direct key access is an explicit failure
mode, not a condition covered by the certificate.
